### What this reference poster is “doing” (and why it works)

- **Single visual spine**: the rocket plume is a thick, central column that your eye treats as the *truth line*. Everything else is commentary. This is the core trick to steal.
- **Alternating callouts**: milestones jump left/right in a rhythm, so the layout feels balanced even with uneven text lengths.
- **Milestone grammar is consistent**: each item has the same pattern—**date → icon badge → 1–2 sentence caption**. Consistency is what makes “many facts” feel simple.
- **The spine is symbolic, not to-scale**: it *pretends* to be a timeline but it’s really an infographic list. That’s why it remains readable.

### Where this template will clash with your “absurd travel” goal

Your simulator’s punchline is **scale** (time dilation + vast distance). A poster like this is linear and human-scale; if you just map “time elapsed” to the spine, you’ll get either:
- **A mile-long poster** for huge trips, or
- **A totally dishonest scale** that undermines the whole satire/science vibe.

So the key evolution is: keep the **spine + callouts**, but change the spine from “linear timeline” to a **story of compression**.

### The best “big picture” mapping to your poster

Keep the same anatomy, but redefine each part:

- **Spine (rocket plume) → “Trip column”**
  - The column becomes a **journey track** from *Depart* to *Arrive*.
  - It can carry **two synchronized rulers**: *Earth time* and *Ship time* as parallel markings (your whole concept, made visual).

- **Milestone callouts → three types (visually distinct)**
  - **Earth events** (history happening back home while you’re gone)
  - **Space flybys** (objects/regions “passed” in distance space)
  - **You/ship events** (aging, “younger by,” onboard milestones)

The poster becomes powerful when these three compete for attention on the same column: “you blink, Earth rewrites itself.”

### The modern/creative trick: “folds” in the timeline

To preserve absurdity *and* readability, the spine can have **explicit discontinuities**:
- **Fold marks / breaks** labeled like “FAST FORWARD: 12,000 YEARS (EARTH)” while ship advances only days/months.
- **Chapters**: “Human timescale” → “civilization timescale” → “geologic” → “cosmic.”
- **Density shift**: early section has many human events; later sections become sparse and eerie (that emptiness is part of the joke).

This keeps the poster honest: it openly admits it’s compressing time, which supports your “absurdity of combining these” theme instead of hiding it.

### One specific insight from the reference to copy directly

The reference’s best readability move is the **icon-in-badge** for each milestone. For you, that’s how you keep three categories readable without adding UI complexity: the badge shape/color becomes the legend, so you don’t need a big legend.

If we “discuss” in the most productive way: the design decision that matters first is whether your spine is **distance-driven** (mile markers, flybys dominate) or **time-driven** (Earth/ship time markers, history dominates). Everything else flows from that.
